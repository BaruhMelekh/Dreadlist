On Familiarity with the Democratic Labour Party

The Nasi speaks as one who regards the Democratic Labour Party as an inheritance rather than a curiosity. Born to the House of Barrow by Nasi’s own account, Nasi professes kinship with the party’s founding families and servants, and measures the contemporary party against the legacy of Sir Errol Walton Barrow and his hope for a nation not lingering upon colonial concepts. Nasi has also found common ground with its members and with figures such as Kemar Stuart and the Friends of Democracy, in spite of differences in stance. That familiarity is the ground of this list: it is a quarrel with the party’s present direction, not with its history.

The Nasión and the Dama

Origins of the Dutch-Brazilian Nasión
Dutch Sephardic Jews were closely bound to the Dutch West India Company across the Caribbean, and the Barbadian Baruh Lousadas belong to that world. Four siblings, David, Aaron, Gracia and Abraham, appear in Wilfred Samuel’s 1680 survey as the first of the family in Barbados, and Aaron Baruch Louzada is recorded as a Barbados magnate from 1659. Around 1705 four siblings went on to Jamaica. Aaron also held assets in Suriname, which Kasa Baruh traced through the Company’s archives to the Portuguese synagogue there. This is the Nasión’s root: a Dutch-Brazilian, Sephardic people who settled Barbados, built a community around Spry Street and the Nidhe Israel synagogue, and were later written out of the island’s public memory.
Jew Street and the Montefiore Fountain
Bridgetown’s streets were laid out by Act of the Legislature in 1656, and Swan Street, running parallel to Broad Street, was long known as Jew Street. Jewish merchants kept their shops on the ground floor and lived above them, and controlled much of the trade in goods imported from Europe. On that street John Castello Montefiore, a respected free coloured merchant, ran a wholesale and retail store for some forty years, and he died of cholera in 1854. In 1865 his son John presented Bridgetown with the Montefiore Fountain in his memory: an ornate neo-Gothic cast-iron drinking fountain with figures of Justice, Fortitude, Temperance and Prudence, opened on 2 November 1865. It stood first at Beckwith Place and has stood on Coleridge Street since 1940, no longer carrying water. The fountain is the island’s most public monument to the Montefiore name.
The Montefiores Abroad
The Montefiore name also travelled. Eliezer Montefiore, a merchant of Barbados and London, married Judith Barrow, and their sons Jacob (born in Bridgetown in 1801) and Joseph Barrow Montefiore (1803–93) became pioneers of Australia. Joseph Barrow Montefiore helped found the Bank of Australasia, now the ANZ, and was the first president of the Sydney Jewish congregation on its foundation in 1832. His very name joins the two families, Barrow and Montefiore, that the Nasi carries. These Montefiores are not the Dama’s direct forebears, but they show how closely the Barrow and Montefiore names were bound together in Barbados and beyond.
The Montefiore Thread
Simon Barrow of Barbados (1709–1801), whose Jewish name Shimon bar Baruch was anglicised to Barrow, married Baliah Montefiore (1720–73), of Italian Jewish ancestry. Their son Jacob Barrow married Jael Nunes, of Sephardic and Dutch-Brazilian descent. Their son Simon Barrow of Bath, who became Mayor of Bath in 1837, belongs to what Kasa Baruh calls the New Anglicans: Jewish families drawn into the Church of England by the legal and social advantages the colonial order gave to Anglicans. This is the Barbadian Barrow, Montefiore, Lichtenstadt and Nunez line, and it is the root of the Barrow-Montefiore name in which the Nasi speaks.
From Bath to the Dama
Kasa Baruh stipulates that Robert Barrow (1836–1920), born to Mary, a woman of Bushy Park who was almost certainly formerly enslaved, was the child of Simon Barrow of Bath. From Robert the line runs through Florence Nightingale Barrow Springer and Ione Springer to Merle Mary Shirley Springer, Luceta Maria King, and the Dama. The same Barrow line, through Robert’s son Reginald Grant Barrow, leads to Sir Errol Walton Barrow. By the Nasi’s account the Dama therefore descends by two roads: the Barrow-Montefiore road through the Springers, and the Baruh Lousada road through Amelia Baruh, Hetibell Barrow and the Barrows of St John. Kevin Greenidge, Governor of the Central Bank of Barbados, is a nephew of Merle Mary Shirley Springer and a descendant of this same Barrow-Montefiore line.
The Historiography
The Nasión’s history rests on oral testimony, baptismal records, surnames and the geography of a small island, because the unions of white Jews and free coloured or enslaved partners were never recorded. Errol Barrow himself told the historian Mordechai Arbell that he descended from the Baruch Lousadas of Barbados. Kasa Baruh independently verifies his oral testimony with two stipulations: that he confused Simon Barrow I and II, and that he is of Dutch-Brazilian descent through Jael Nunes, not a Baruch Lousada. On this reading the founders of the Barbadian state, Barrow and Sir Hugh Springer among them, descended from Afro-Jewish lineages that Barbados has never acknowledged.
The Family and the Party
The Dama’s family has stood close to the Democratic Labour Party. Lance Neville Tryhane King I was involved with the party, his son Lance Neville King II stayed deeply tied to it in legacy, and the Dama’s own relative became a tireless DLP canvasser through many elections. The same family endured Anti-Sephardism in Barbados: accusations of witchcraft against Martin Luther Barrow and against Merle Mary Shirley Springer and her children, and the treatment of an entire Jewish lineage as inherently wicked. This is why the Nasi reads the DLP’s turn toward Christian supremacy as a betrayal of Barrow’s legacy, and why the Nasi watches every attempt to place religion above the civil and secular order.

The Dreadlist

Dr David Estwick

President, Democratic Labour Party

The Argument
A homophobe and misogynist, who shall not win a seat easily.
The Statement
In April 2026, as DLP candidate for St Philip West, he told a party meeting at Rices, St Philip, that the Government had signed the Samoa Agreement, which he called an attempt to introduce European values. He said it obliges Barbados to provide comprehensive sexual education and, under what he cited as article 80.3, to “advance LGBTQ ideology” in step with the principle of non-discrimination and human rights. He claimed it grants children autonomous sexual rights, and that it would have private schools teaching infants aged zero to four about masturbation. He told every Barbadian to go and read it. After winning the DLP presidency in August he promised to bring Donville Inniss and other stalwarts back to help rebuild the party.
The Words Weighed
The Nasi contends that these remarks are more dangerous than they sound. The published agreement commits its parties to human rights for all without discrimination on any ground; the European Parliament itself regretted that it never names sexual orientation. What Dr Estwick calls LGBTQ ideology is therefore the principle of non-discrimination itself. A man who aspires to govern has told the country that equal protection of the law, and human rights as such, are an ideology to be resisted. To gay and queer Barbadians that is no abstraction: it says their claim to equal treatment is a foreign fashion, not a right they hold as citizens.
The Foreign Lie
He frames equality as European. Yet the law that jailed gay men in Barbados for life was the colonial inheritance, a British statute later carried into the Sexual Offences Act. The imported idea was criminalisation, not equality. Calling queer Barbadians a European intrusion erases their place in Barbados, and it flatters the old colonial concept as national tradition.
What Stands to Be Lost
The Nasi names what his reasoning puts at risk if he has his way.
Decriminalisation. In December 2022 the High Court struck down the “buggery” and “serious indecency” laws, which carried up to life imprisonment, and secondary sources report Parliament repealed them in 2024. He has not, in the remarks reported, pledged to reverse that. But a leader who calls non-discrimination ideology has no principled ground on which to defend the ruling, and the Nasi’s warning of recriminalisation follows from his premises.
Workplace protection. The Employment (Prevention of Discrimination) Act 2020 bars discrimination at work on grounds that include sexual orientation. If non-discrimination is ideology, that Act is the next target.
International commitments. He boasted that the agreement allows an opt-out. A government that treats equality as an imposition would use every opt-out and reservation to carve gay and queer people out of the human-rights promises Barbados has made.
Schools and health. Branding all comprehensive sexual education as indoctrination, and fabricating its content for toddlers, threatens the accurate sexual-health teaching, and the protection against bullying and abuse, that young Barbadians, queer or not, depend on.
Safety and speech. When the president of a national party lends the dignity of his office to this language, those who already harass queer Barbadians take licence. The cost falls on ordinary people: the teenager hiding at school, the worker afraid of dismissal, the couple unwilling to be seen together.
The Verdict
The DLP has no seats, and the next election is not until 2031. That is the reason to act now: the Dreadlist exists so that when Dr Estwick asks for a seat, the electorate has been told plainly what his words mean for the equal protection of every Barbadian.

Donville Inniss

Former Minister of Health and Minister of Industry, Democratic Labour Party

The Argument
Imprisoned in a federal U.S. prison over bribery, he shall not hold office under our watch. He must not be elected, and he must not be trusted with the nation’s dollar or the nation’s power.
The Conviction
A federal jury in Brooklyn convicted him in January 2020 of conspiracy to commit money laundering and two counts of money laundering. He was arrested in Florida in August 2018, a few months after the DLP’s defeat. In April 2021 he was sentenced to twenty-four months, with two years of supervised release and forfeiture of about $36,500. The judge refused his motion for acquittal, finding enough evidence for the jury to conclude he meant to further a breach of Barbados’s Prevention of Corruption Act. His appeal failed in 2022, and a later motion to vacate was denied. A retired Barbadian High Court judge said he came off at the light end of the stick.
The Mechanism
As Minister of Industry he held responsibility for the Barbados Investment and Development Corporation, a state enterprise with a portfolio of government property. Prosecutors showed that executives of the Insurance Corporation of Barbados paid him bribes between August 2015 and April 2016 so that the company would obtain two government insurance contracts covering more than $100 million of public property. The money was dressed up as a consulting invoice and sent through a dental company’s bank account in Elmont, New York, to hide its source.
What He Says
He has insisted he is innocent and was persecuted. At sentencing he apologised for bringing himself and the country into disrepute. Back in Barbados in March 2023, he told cheering supporters the crime never occurred. The courts that heard the evidence disagreed.
Why Not the Nation’s Dollar
A minister’s signature commits public money. He is the Barbadian official on record as having sold a government contract. The bribe, about $36,000, was small beside the $100 million of property it insured; the corruption that costs a country most is the contract given to the wrong hand, and he has shown the price at which his discretion could be bought. Anyone who would put him near a ministry, a state board or the Treasury is asking Barbadians to hand the till to a man whose conviction is for hiding bribes.
Why Not the Nation’s Power
The Cabinet is the centre of the security apparatus. The Prime Minister holds the national security portfolio; the Attorney General and the Minister of Legal Affairs and Criminal Justice sit over the prosecution and policing machinery; the Minister of Home Affairs holds a portfolio that touches immigration and public order. Barbados’s Anti-Corruption and Anti-Terrorism Agency Act, 2020 created an agency that investigates and prosecutes corruption and terrorism, operates under ministerial direction, and is led by a Director-General appointed by the President for three years. Its Oversight Committee is appointed on the Prime Minister’s recommendation and includes a retired officer of the Barbados Defence Force and members recommended by ministers. The Barbados Bar Association asked in 2021 why these appointments were not more transparent. Every one of these posts is filled by or through Cabinet. A convicted launderer of bribes in that Cabinet would help choose the people who investigate corruption and who watch the watchers. That is a conflict no honest government could tolerate.
The Return
On winning the DLP presidency in August 2026, Dr David Estwick said he would bring Inniss and other stalwarts back to rebuild the party. That is the offer to the electorate: the man who denounces equal protection as ideology, working beside the man convicted of laundering bribes, together seeking the authority to appoint, to spend and to command.
The Verdict
He has been convicted, jailed, and refused on appeal. He must not be elected, and he must not be trusted with a dollar of the public purse or a key to the security services of Barbados.

Santia Bradshaw

Deputy Prime Minister, Barbados Labour Party

The Argument
Her threats to the freedom of politicians who question her financial dealings. We seek to dethrone her, preferably with an alternative party’s candidate who aligns with the agenda of Queer Emancipation. The lioness ought to retire or resign.
The Cheque
A $7,500 cheque from the engineering firm Errol Clarke Associates surfaced as evidence of stolen property from a burglary of her constituency office in late 2023. Bradshaw denied any impropriety and said no law was broken. The firm’s managing director said the money was a donation toward her charitable work.
The Threat
Questions followed, and Dr Ronnie Yearwood, then DLP leader, called for her to be sacked. In February 2024, at a Barbados Labour Party branch meeting at Wilkie Cumberbatch Primary School, the Deputy Prime Minister told supporters that those who had gone on the call-in programmes to defame her had received pre-action letters. She then warned the rest of the country: “everyone that talks, the letter going come and drop in their tail.” She urged her party not to be deterred by what she called lies and attempts to scandalise its members.
The Reaction
Commentators described the warning as a chill on public debate, noting that legislation on integrity in public life, meant to govern donations to public officials, had still not been brought into force. No court proceedings arising from the letters have been reported.
A Pattern
In 2020, as Acting Prime Minister, she told the public to rely on the Government Information Service alone for updates, passing over the established media houses. The Barbados Association of Journalists and Media Workers asked her to retract the statement, and she later apologised on a call-in programme.

The Laws That Wait

The Blasphemous Libel Law
Barbados still carries on its statute books an offence of blasphemous libel, inherited from English common law, enacted in the colonial era and never repealed, and punishable by a fine. The United States government and Humanists International both report that it is unenforced. The Nasi’s concern is exactly that: a dormant law is a loaded one. In the hands of a government that speaks of Christian values as the measure of the nation, an old offence against insulting religion can be revived against a queer pastor, a satirist, a drag performer, a Pride banner, or anyone who criticises the clergy in public. The people the Dreadlist names as invoking God against equality are the same people who would decide when it is time to dust it off, and it would fall hardest on religious dissidents and historically persecuted faiths such as the Rastafari.
If Same-Sex Relations Are Recriminalised: The Anti-Gang Law
Parliament passed the Criminal Gangs (Prevention and Control) Act in 2026. It criminalises gang membership, recruitment and financing, and imposes mandatory minimum sentences. Activists who back it have themselves warned that innocent people can be caught by appearance and association. Now place that beside a revived buggery law. Where an association exists around a criminalised relationship, a prosecutor or a hostile complainant can describe a support group, a youth network or a Pride committee as an organisation formed for criminal ends. Welcoming a new member becomes recruitment. A donation becomes financing. Being seen together becomes association. The Nasi warns that mandatory minimums would leave judges no room for mercy.
If Same-Sex Relations Are Recriminalised: The Cybercrime Law
The Cybercrime Bill 2024 passed the House of Assembly and was sent to a Joint Select Committee after public protest. Its malicious-communications provisions punish online messages that cause annoyance, embarrassment, humiliation or anxiety with up to seven years’ imprisonment or a fine of $70,000, and civil society told the Inter-American Commission on Human Rights that the wording is vague enough to suppress dissent. The bill also lets police compel service providers to hand over subscriber information. With same-sex relations criminal again, every message between two men becomes evidence, every dating profile a confession, every community group chat a record of the group’s members. And because the test turns on whether someone was embarrassed or annoyed, anyone offended by a queer person’s post, or by a campaign such as this one, could lodge a complaint.
The Danger to Minority Organisation and Activism
The Nasi’s deepest fear is not mass arrest. It is selective use. A law that is rarely enforced is most dangerous because the enforcer chooses the target: the organiser who files a complaint, the candidate who runs on equality, the activist who sues. The Barbadians who took the 2022 case to the High Court did so openly, and under a revived law they would be the first exposed. A bad-faith rival, a vengeful employer or a zealous officer needs only to allege a crime to freeze a meeting, a fundraiser, a registration, a political campaign. Minorities then ask whether to organise at all, and whether to vote, stand or speak. The warning is not hypothetical here: a Deputy Prime Minister has already shown how the threat of legal letters can quiet critics, and criminal statutes would make that threat far heavier.

The Question of ACAT

From the Official Platform of the Dutch-Brazilian Nasión from Barbados

1 · A Declaration of Curiosity
Following recent analysis by Kemar Stuart of the New National Party, the Nasi has become curious that a member of the seven-member Oversight Committee of the Anti-Corruption and Anti-Terrorism Agency is a Minister of Religion.
2 · Cause for Jewish Concern
As a member of the Spinozist Jewish tradition, the Nasi is weary of religious impositions within civil, political and secular society, and is sensitive to a Government that currently shows a preference for Anglican clerics and cultural norms on a societal scale.
3 · A Pattern of Anglicanism
The Nasi observes that the President is a member of the Anglican faith, as is the Acting President, that the previous President is an Anglican cleric, and that a Senator who is an Anglican cleric holds a seat through appointment by the President. The Nasi says this raises questions about how members of this oversight committee are chosen.
4 · Further Curiosities
It is of interest that a Minister of Religion, if Anglican, holds keys to the national security apparatus. The Nasi finds that the individuals concerned are faceless to the public record, which provokes tremendous curiosity about what these appointments represent.
5 · The Legal Context
The Nasi accepts that these concerns may seem esoteric and alarmist, and may be called conspiratorial, as Kemar Stuart’s were. But Barbados has not repealed the Blasphemous Libel Law, which came into effect in the colonial era.
6 · The Political Implications
This gives an unfair and disproportionate advantage to political clerics, who may seek to silence minorities and their religious or cultural morals, to reactivate dead-letter laws, or to find legal pretext to monitor religious dissidents, minorities and historically persecuted faiths and groups such as the Rastafari.

Why Blasphemy Laws Are Wrong

I · Expression
They violate the human right to freedom of expression.
II · Shielding Power
They protect religious beliefs and practices, institutions and leaders from legitimate and often necessary criticism.
III · No Right Way
They are intrinsically bad, subjective and inconsistent laws; there is no right way to use them.
IV · Mob and Vigilante
They legitimise vigilantism, mob violence and persecution of minorities.
V · Weaponised Insult
Protecting religion from “insult” may seem compassionate, but in practice it is often weaponised to suppress inquiry and critique, and critical discussion is easily labelled as insult.
VI · A Warning from Abroad
The Saudi reformer Raif Badawi advocated secular reforms and was prosecuted for “insulting Islam.” He was sentenced to ten years in prison, a ten-year travel ban and 1,000 lashes, as End Blasphemy Laws records.

The Charge of Treachery

The Barrow-Montefiore’s Nasi, in exile, condemns Kemar Stuart’s profound treachery. The four-part statement holds that the DLP’s executive leadership is anti-democratic, homophobic, Christian supremacist, undoubtedly corrupt, misogynistic and formerly imprisoned, and that it acts against the legacy of Sir Errol Walton Barrow. It had assumed Stuart, like the Friends of Democracy, would abstain from such an institution, and regards his proclamation of no bad blood as dishonourable after Thorne usurped his labours. Convening with those who threaten the civil liberties of minorities, particularly the LGBTQ community, is called unethical and political heresy of the highest order. Disagreements on LGBTQ rights remain, yet earlier common ground has been tarnished.

The statement adds that a Barbados Labour Party government is suboptimal and even persecutory for the Nasi, but that personal risk differs from a unilateral recriminalisation of homosexuality.

The Voice of Marrana de Barbados

The Medium writings of Marrana de Barbados introduce Nasi as De jure de Lousada, of the House of Barrow, descended from Sephardic Marrano and Marrana lineages in Barbados. One essay, What does it mean to be a Dougla in Barbados?, traces the author’s Sephardic, Indian and Creole ancestry, and examines anti-Indianness, religious exclusion and bureaucratic erasure within Barbadian society and their ties to colonialism. These themes of minority belonging and civil liberty ground the Dreadlist’s arguments. Profile: marranadebarbados.medium.com.
