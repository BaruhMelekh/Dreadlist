On Familiarity with the Democratic Labour Party

The Nasi speaks as one who regards the Democratic Labour Party as an inheritance rather than a curiosity. Born to the House of Barrow by Nasi’s own account, Nasi professes kinship with the party’s founding families and servants, and measures the contemporary party against the legacy of Sir Errol Walton Barrow and his hope for a nation not lingering upon colonial concepts. Nasi has also found common ground with its members and with figures such as Kemar Stuart and the Friends of Democracy, in spite of differences in stance. That familiarity is the ground of this list: it is a quarrel with the party’s present direction, not with its history.

Service to the Party

The Nasi served as Convener of the Research and Education Committee of the Young Democrats, a body informally part of the Public Relations Committee of the Democratic Labour Party under Dr Ronnie Yearwood.

In that office the Nasi was responsible for several overt and covert digital activities on the party’s behalf.

The Dismantling

By the time of Ralph Thorne’s return to the Party, the Nasi’s committee had been thoroughly dismantled, on the advice of Dr Yearwood’s advisers within the party. This marginalised the Nasi, and the Queer movement within the party, to its far margins.

Despite the dissolution of the committee, the Nasi continued to work closely with Felicia Dujon, conducting parliamentary research and other public relations tasks.

The Resignation

Upon Ralph Thorne’s proclamation of an intention to recriminalise homosexuality, the Dama formally resigned from the party and cut all ties.

The Campaign of 2025

A campaign in 2025 against Ralph Thorne and the Democratic Labour Party ruffled the feathers of the Barbados Labour Party and, by extension, EQUALS. From that point onward, open or covert hostility and queer rivalries arose between the Nasi and those of EQUALS and the Barbados Labour Party.

Threats from Within

Some persons affiliated with the Democratic Labour Party openly declared that the Nasi would be murdered. This always occurred in the context of Christianity and the Nasi’s resistance to it in favour of secularism.

The Nasión and the Dama

Origins of the Dutch-Brazilian Nasión

Dutch Sephardic Jews were closely bound to the Dutch West India Company across the Caribbean, and the Barbadian Baruh Lousadas belong to that world.

Four siblings, David, Aaron, Gracia and Abraham, appear in Wilfred Samuel’s 1680 survey as the first of the family in Barbados, and Aaron Baruch Louzada is recorded as a Barbados magnate from 1659.

Around 1705 four siblings went on to Jamaica. Aaron also held assets in Suriname, which Kasa Baruh traced through the Company’s archives to the Portuguese synagogue there.

This is the Nasión’s root: a Dutch-Brazilian, Sephardic people who settled Barbados, built a community around Spry Street and the Nidhe Israel synagogue, and were later written out of the island’s public memory.

Jew Street and the Montefiore Fountain

Bridgetown’s streets were laid out by Act of the Legislature in 1656, and Swan Street, running parallel to Broad Street, was long known as Jew Street.

Jewish merchants kept their shops on the ground floor and lived above them, and controlled much of the trade in goods imported from Europe.

On that street John Castello Montefiore, a respected free coloured merchant, ran a wholesale and retail store for some forty years, and he died of cholera in 1854.

In 1865 his son John presented Bridgetown with the Montefiore Fountain in his memory: an ornate neo-Gothic cast-iron drinking fountain with figures of Justice, Fortitude, Temperance and Prudence, opened on 2 November 1865.

It stood first at Beckwith Place and has stood on Coleridge Street since 1940, no longer carrying water. The fountain is the island’s most public monument to the Montefiore name.

The Montefiores Abroad

The Montefiore name also travelled. Eliezer Montefiore, a merchant of Barbados and London, married Judith Barrow, and their sons Jacob (born in Bridgetown in 1801) and Joseph Barrow Montefiore (1803–93) became pioneers of Australia.

Joseph Barrow Montefiore helped found the Bank of Australasia, now the ANZ, and was the first president of the Sydney Jewish congregation on its foundation in 1832.

His very name joins the two families, Barrow and Montefiore, that the Nasi carries. These Montefiores are not the Dama’s direct forebears, but they show how closely the Barrow and Montefiore names were bound together in Barbados and beyond.

The Montefiore Thread

Simon Barrow of Barbados (1709–1801), whose Jewish name Shimon bar Baruch was anglicised to Barrow, married Baliah Montefiore (1720–73), of Italian Jewish ancestry.

Their son Jacob Barrow married Jael Nunes, of Sephardic and Dutch-Brazilian descent.

Their son Simon Barrow of Bath, who became Mayor of Bath in 1837, belongs to what Kasa Baruh calls the New Anglicans: Jewish families drawn into the Church of England by the legal and social advantages the colonial order gave to Anglicans.

This is the Barbadian Barrow, Montefiore, Lichtenstadt and Nunez line, and it is the root of the Barrow-Montefiore name in which the Nasi speaks.

From Bath to the Dama

Kasa Baruh stipulates that Robert Barrow (1836–1920), born to Mary, a woman of Bushy Park who was almost certainly formerly enslaved, was the child of Simon Barrow of Bath.

From Robert the line runs through Florence Nightingale Barrow Springer and Ione Springer to Merle Mary Shirley Springer, Luceta Maria King, and the Dama.

The same Barrow line, through Robert’s son Reginald Grant Barrow, leads to Sir Errol Walton Barrow.

By the Nasi’s account the Dama therefore descends by two roads: the Barrow-Montefiore road through the Springers, and the Baruh Lousada road through Amelia Baruh, Hetibell Barrow and the Barrows of St John.

Kevin Greenidge, Governor of the Central Bank of Barbados, is a nephew of Merle Mary Shirley Springer and a descendant of this same Barrow-Montefiore line.

The Historiography

The Nasión’s history rests on oral testimony, baptismal records, surnames and the geography of a small island, because the unions of white Jews and free coloured or enslaved partners were never recorded.

Errol Barrow himself told the historian Mordechai Arbell that he descended from the Baruch Lousadas of Barbados.

Kasa Baruh independently verifies his oral testimony with two stipulations: that he confused Simon Barrow I and II, and that he is of Dutch-Brazilian descent through Jael Nunes, not a Baruch Lousada.

On this reading the founders of the Barbadian state, Barrow and Sir Hugh Springer among them, descended from Afro-Jewish lineages that Barbados has never acknowledged.

The Family and the Party

The Dama’s family has stood close to the Democratic Labour Party.

Lance Neville Trehane King I was involved with the party, his son Lance Neville King II stayed deeply tied to it in legacy, and the Dama’s own relative became a tireless DLP canvasser through many elections.

The same family endured Anti-Sephardism in Barbados: accusations of witchcraft against Martin Luther Barrow and against Merle Mary Shirley Springer and her children, and the treatment of an entire Jewish lineage as inherently wicked.

This is why the Nasi reads the DLP’s turn toward Christian supremacy as a betrayal of Barrow’s legacy, and why the Nasi watches every attempt to place religion above the civil and secular order.

The Dreadlist

Dr David Estwick

President, Democratic Labour Party

In short: Calls equal rights “LGBTQ ideology.” If that thinking wins power, protections for gay and queer Bajans are at risk.

The Argument

A homophobe and misogynist, who shall not win a seat easily.

Read the full case
The Statement

In April 2026, as DLP candidate for St Philip West, he told a party meeting at Rices, St Philip, that the Government had signed the Samoa Agreement, which he called an attempt to introduce European values.

He said it obliges Barbados to provide comprehensive sexual education and, under what he cited as article 80.3, to “advance LGBTQ ideology” in step with the principle of non-discrimination and human rights.

He claimed it grants children autonomous sexual rights, and that it would have private schools teaching infants aged zero to four about masturbation. He told every Barbadian to go and read it.

After winning the DLP presidency in August he promised to bring Donville Inniss and other stalwarts back to help rebuild the party.

The Words Weighed

The Nasi contends that these remarks are more dangerous than they sound.

The published agreement commits its parties to human rights for all without discrimination on any ground; the European Parliament itself regretted that it never names sexual orientation.

What Dr Estwick calls LGBTQ ideology is therefore the principle of non-discrimination itself.

A man who aspires to govern has told the country that equal protection of the law, and human rights as such, are an ideology to be resisted.

To gay and queer Barbadians that is no abstraction: it says their claim to equal treatment is a foreign fashion, not a right they hold as citizens.

The Foreign Lie

He frames equality as European. Yet the law that jailed gay men in Barbados for life was the colonial inheritance, a British statute later carried into the Sexual Offences Act. The imported idea was criminalisation, not equality.

Calling queer Barbadians a European intrusion erases their place in Barbados, and it flatters the old colonial concept as national tradition.

What Stands to Be Lost

The Nasi names what his reasoning puts at risk if he has his way.
Decriminalisation. In December 2022 the High Court struck down the “buggery” and “serious indecency” laws, which carried up to life imprisonment, and secondary sources report Parliament repealed them in 2024. He has not, in the remarks reported, pledged to reverse that. But a leader who calls non-discrimination ideology has no principled ground on which to defend the ruling, and the Nasi’s warning of recriminalisation follows from his premises.
Workplace protection. The Employment (Prevention of Discrimination) Act 2020 bars discrimination at work on grounds that include sexual orientation. If non-discrimination is ideology, that Act is the next target.
International commitments. He boasted that the agreement allows an opt-out. A government that treats equality as an imposition would use every opt-out and reservation to carve gay and queer people out of the human-rights promises Barbados has made.
Schools and health. Branding all comprehensive sexual education as indoctrination, and fabricating its content for toddlers, threatens the accurate sexual-health teaching, and the protection against bullying and abuse, that young Barbadians, queer or not, depend on.
Safety and speech. When the president of a national party lends the dignity of his office to this language, those who already harass queer Barbadians take licence. The cost falls on ordinary people: the teenager hiding at school, the worker afraid of dismissal, the couple unwilling to be seen together.

The Verdict

The DLP has no seats, and the next election is not until 2031.

That is the reason to act now: the Dreadlist exists so that when Dr Estwick asks for a seat, the electorate has been told plainly what his words mean for the equal protection of every Barbadian.

Donville Inniss

Former Minister of Health and Minister of Industry, Democratic Labour Party

In short: Convicted in the US of laundering bribes tied to government contracts. Should not touch public money or power.

The Argument

Imprisoned in a federal U.S. prison over bribery, he shall not hold office under our watch. He must not be elected, and he must not be trusted with the nation’s dollar or the nation’s power.

Read the full case
The Conviction

A federal jury in Brooklyn convicted him in January 2020 of conspiracy to commit money laundering and two counts of money laundering. He was arrested in Florida in August 2018, a few months after the DLP’s defeat.

In April 2021 he was sentenced to twenty-four months, with two years of supervised release and forfeiture of about $36,500.

The judge refused his motion for acquittal, finding enough evidence for the jury to conclude he meant to further a breach of Barbados’s Prevention of Corruption Act. His appeal failed in 2022, and a later motion to vacate was denied.

A retired Barbadian High Court judge said he came off at the light end of the stick.

The Mechanism

As Minister of Industry he held responsibility for the Barbados Investment and Development Corporation, a state enterprise with a portfolio of government property.

Prosecutors showed that executives of the Insurance Corporation of Barbados paid him bribes between August 2015 and April 2016 so that the company would obtain two government insurance contracts covering more than $100 million of public property.

The money was dressed up as a consulting invoice and sent through a dental company’s bank account in Elmont, New York, to hide its source.

What He Says

He has insisted he is innocent and was persecuted. At sentencing he apologised for bringing himself and the country into disrepute. Back in Barbados in March 2023, he told cheering supporters the crime never occurred.

The courts that heard the evidence disagreed.

Why Not the Nation’s Dollar

A minister’s signature commits public money. He is the Barbadian official on record as having sold a government contract.

The bribe, about $36,000, was small beside the $100 million of property it insured; the corruption that costs a country most is the contract given to the wrong hand, and he has shown the price at which his discretion could be bought.

Anyone who would put him near a ministry, a state board or the Treasury is asking Barbadians to hand the till to a man whose conviction is for hiding bribes.

Why Not the Nation’s Power

The Cabinet is the centre of the security apparatus.

The Prime Minister holds the national security portfolio; the Attorney General and the Minister of Legal Affairs and Criminal Justice sit over the prosecution and policing machinery; the Minister of Home Affairs holds a portfolio that touches immigration and public order.

Barbados’s Anti-Corruption and Anti-Terrorism Agency Act, 2020 created an agency that investigates and prosecutes corruption and terrorism, operates under ministerial direction, and is led by a Director-General appointed by the President for three years.

Its Oversight Committee is appointed on the Prime Minister’s recommendation and includes a retired officer of the Barbados Defence Force and members recommended by ministers.

The Barbados Bar Association asked in 2021 why these appointments were not more transparent. Every one of these posts is filled by or through Cabinet.

A convicted launderer of bribes in that Cabinet would help choose the people who investigate corruption and who watch the watchers. That is a conflict no honest government could tolerate.

The Return

On winning the DLP presidency in August 2026, Dr David Estwick said he would bring Inniss and other stalwarts back to rebuild the party.

That is the offer to the electorate: the man who denounces equal protection as ideology, working beside the man convicted of laundering bribes, together seeking the authority to appoint, to spend and to command.

The Verdict

He has been convicted, jailed, and refused on appeal. He must not be elected, and he must not be trusted with a dollar of the public purse or a key to the security services of Barbados.

The Laws That Wait

The Blasphemous Libel Law

Barbados still carries on its statute books an offence of blasphemous libel, inherited from English common law, enacted in the colonial era and never repealed, and punishable by a fine.

The United States government and Humanists International both report that it is unenforced. The Nasi’s concern is exactly that: a dormant law is a loaded one.

In the hands of a government that speaks of Christian values as the measure of the nation, an old offence against insulting religion can be revived against a queer pastor, a satirist, a drag performer, a Pride banner, or anyone who criticises the clergy in public.

The people the Dreadlist names as invoking God against equality are the same people who would decide when it is time to dust it off, and it would fall hardest on religious dissidents and historically persecuted faiths such as the Rastafari.

If Same-Sex Relations Are Recriminalised: The Anti-Gang Law

Parliament passed the Criminal Gangs (Prevention and Control) Act in 2026. It criminalises gang membership, recruitment and financing, and imposes mandatory minimum sentences.

Activists who back it have themselves warned that innocent people can be caught by appearance and association. Now place that beside a revived buggery law.

Where an association exists around a criminalised relationship, a prosecutor or a hostile complainant can describe a support group, a youth network or a Pride committee as an organisation formed for criminal ends.

Welcoming a new member becomes recruitment. A donation becomes financing. Being seen together becomes association. The Nasi warns that mandatory minimums would leave judges no room for mercy.

If Same-Sex Relations Are Recriminalised: The Cybercrime Law

The Cybercrime Bill 2024 passed the House of Assembly and was sent to a Joint Select Committee after public protest.

Its malicious-communications provisions punish online messages that cause annoyance, embarrassment, humiliation or anxiety with up to seven years’ imprisonment or a fine of $70,000, and civil society told the Inter-American Commission on Human Rights that the wording is vague enough to suppress dissent.

The bill also lets police compel service providers to hand over subscriber information.

With same-sex relations criminal again, every message between two men becomes evidence, every dating profile a confession, every community group chat a record of the group’s members.

And because the test turns on whether someone was embarrassed or annoyed, anyone offended by a queer person’s post, or by a campaign such as this one, could lodge a complaint.

The Danger to Minority Organisation and Activism

The Nasi’s deepest fear is not mass arrest. It is selective use.

A law that is rarely enforced is most dangerous because the enforcer chooses the target: the organiser who files a complaint, the candidate who runs on equality, the activist who sues.

The Barbadians who took the 2022 case to the High Court did so openly, and under a revived law they would be the first exposed.

A bad-faith rival, a vengeful employer or a zealous officer needs only to allege a crime to freeze a meeting, a fundraiser, a registration, a political campaign.

Minorities then ask whether to organise at all, and whether to vote, stand or speak.

The warning is not hypothetical here: a Deputy Prime Minister has already shown how the threat of legal letters can quiet critics, and criminal statutes would make that threat far heavier.

The Question of ACAT

From the Official Platform of the Dutch-Brazilian Nasión from Barbados

1 · A Declaration of Curiosity

Following recent analysis by Kemar Stuart of the New National Party, the Nasi has become curious that a member of the seven-member Oversight Committee of the Anti-Corruption and Anti-Terrorism Agency is a Minister of Religion.

2 · Cause for Jewish Concern

As a member of the Spinozist Jewish tradition, the Nasi is weary of religious impositions within civil, political and secular society, and is sensitive to a Government that currently shows a preference for Anglican clerics and cultural norms on a societal scale.

3 · A Pattern of Anglicanism

The Nasi observes that the President is a member of the Anglican faith, as is the Acting President, that the previous President is an Anglican cleric, and that a Senator who is an Anglican cleric holds a seat through appointment by the President.

The Nasi says this raises questions about how members of this oversight committee are chosen.

4 · Further Curiosities

It is of interest that a Minister of Religion, if Anglican, holds keys to the national security apparatus.

The Nasi finds that the individuals concerned are faceless to the public record, which provokes tremendous curiosity about what these appointments represent.

5 · The Legal Context

The Nasi accepts that these concerns may seem esoteric and alarmist, and may be called conspiratorial, as Kemar Stuart’s were. But Barbados has not repealed the Blasphemous Libel Law, which came into effect in the colonial era.

6 · The Political Implications

This gives an unfair and disproportionate advantage to political clerics, who may seek to silence minorities and their religious or cultural morals, to reactivate dead-letter laws, or to find legal pretext to monitor religious dissidents, minorities and historically persecuted faiths and groups such as the Rastafari.

Why Blasphemy Laws Are Wrong

I · Expression

They violate the human right to freedom of expression.

II · Shielding Power

They protect religious beliefs and practices, institutions and leaders from legitimate and often necessary criticism.

III · No Right Way

They are intrinsically bad, subjective and inconsistent laws; there is no right way to use them.

IV · Mob and Vigilante

They legitimise vigilantism, mob violence and persecution of minorities.

V · Weaponised Insult

Protecting religion from “insult” may seem compassionate, but in practice it is often weaponised to suppress inquiry and critique, and critical discussion is easily labelled as insult.

VI · A Warning from Abroad

The Saudi reformer Raif Badawi advocated secular reforms and was prosecuted for “insulting Islam.” He was sentenced to ten years in prison, a ten-year travel ban and 1,000 lashes, as End Blasphemy Laws records.

The Charge of Treachery

The Barrow-Montefiore’s Nasi, in exile, condemns Kemar Stuart’s profound treachery. The four-part statement holds that the DLP’s executive leadership is anti-democratic, homophobic, Christian supremacist, undoubtedly corrupt, misogynistic and formerly imprisoned, and that it acts against the legacy of Sir Errol Walton Barrow. It had assumed Stuart, like the Friends of Democracy, would abstain from such an institution, and regards his proclamation of no bad blood as dishonourable after Thorne usurped his labours. Convening with those who threaten the civil liberties of minorities, particularly the LGBTQ community, is called unethical and political heresy of the highest order. Disagreements on LGBTQ rights remain, yet earlier common ground has been tarnished.

The statement adds that a Barbados Labour Party government is suboptimal and even persecutory for the Nasi, but that personal risk differs from a unilateral recriminalisation of homosexuality.

Political Biography of the Nasi

Kasa Baruh, Dama di Louzada, Nasi of the Dutch-Brazilian Nasión from Barbados, writes as a refugee. This political biography records, in the Nasi’s own account, the events that brought the Dreadlist into being. Matters bearing on the Democratic Labour Party are set out under “On Familiarity with the Democratic Labour Party.”

A Political Family

The Nasi comes from a political family. Lance Neville Trehane King I, the father of Lance Neville King II, and the cousins of Ione Springer were a political people.

Lance Neville Trehane King I and Sir Errol Walton Barrow both served in the Second World War, King at sea and Barrow in the air.

Sir Hugh Springer, Sir Errol Walton Barrow and many other Springers and Barrows took part in active party politics, most especially in the Democratic Labour Party, which for one reason or another was popular with the Eurafrican Jewish community.

Lance Neville King II inherited the remnants of this culture. Given his treatment, however, his children were burnt out and disappointed by the party, by its neglect and by its poor treatment of its stalwarts, and shifted to the Barbados Labour Party.

The Nasi stood as the sole exception.

The Roots of a Political Life

In 2023 the Nasi came to hold dearly a queer lover, and it was through that attachment that the Nasi became politically inclined. The service of the Nasi’s grandfather, Neville King (Lance Neville King II), further moved the Nasi to choose the Democratic Labour Party in particular.

The Nasi’s grandfather was awarded for forty years of service by Freundel Stuart during his administration.

1 · EQUALS and the BYDC

The Nasi gained entry to the BYDC through the Young Democrats, where the Nasi spoke positively of Tyra Trotman’s efforts. The Nasi became an informal liaison between the BYDC and EQUALS, a role that became more central after the Nasi’s formal exit from the Democratic Labour Party.

Following the resignation from the Democratic Labour Party, the Nasi worked closely with EQUALS and the BYDC, and for a brief period served as Editorial Lead. Disputes over the publication of EQUALS’ achievements permanently soured the Nasi’s relationship with its leadership.

Many of those involved have since remained cordial with the Nasi, subject to successive scandals and rivalries. Caleb Braithwaite, in particular, threatened legal action against the Nasi for speaking too boldly at his expense. The matter concerned the unpaid labour of youths working in the BYDC and the overworking of Kemara Harris.

Having formally severed ties with the BYDC, the Nasi resolved to remain within the sphere of EQUALS, and confided to many the Nasi’s concerns about what the Democratic Labour Party had become. The campaign of 2025 against Ralph Thorne and the Democratic Labour Party then incurred the displeasure of the Barbados Labour Party and, by extension, EQUALS. From that point open and covert hostility and queer rivalries followed.

2 · The Trim Dispute and the Poetic Feud

It was articulated to the Nasi that the Nasi’s form of advocacy could bring danger to the queer community of Barbados. The Nasi was denied a platform on the League of Young Socialists podcast, with Roshanna Trim citing “public backlash,” and the Dama stated that the Dama would remember Trim’s refusal.

Roshanna Trim confided in the matter with her brother, Rondell Trim, who subsequently issued threats on her behalf, stating that the Dama could, or would, be blacklisted from Government employment for her proclivities.

This occurred at an event hosted by EQUALS, with Raven of Butterfly Barbados present. The Dama felt bullied and chose, though briefly, to conform with the assimilationist policies of EQUALS and its leadership. This did not last, and the Dama eventually professed that Rondell and Roshanna Trim would one day be forced to “kiss the ring of the Lousada.”

The ensuing conflict became well known in some circles, and many queer people took sides, usually at the Dama’s expense.

The Dama accused other queer poets of covertly taking the Dama’s fashion-photo-shoot poetry, and was distressed that those poets received more likes and support on Instagram within the queer community.

The poetic feud persists to this day.

3 · Refuge in the Jewish Community of Barbados

These pressures led the Nasi to seek refuge in the Jewish Community of Barbados. The Nasi was appalled by that community’s management of the Nasi’s heritage, and withdrew into insularity for at least a year, rarely leaving the house and keeping almost exclusively to a very large family across Bridgetown.

This too came to an end. The Nasi’s politics, Jewish identity and Sephardic linguistic traits caused the family great concern for its reputation, though a number of the young Lousadas were inspired to reclaim Spanish.

4 · The Family and the Reckoning

The Barrow-Montefiore were the least pleased of all. Several found the Nasi’s political inclinations unbearable to their daily lives, and although the Nasi’s proclamation of Jewishness was already known, not every member was willing to engage with it.

Conflicts with the Barbados Labour Party, EQUALS, the Jewish Community, the Democratic Labour Party, conspiracy theorists and queerphobes strained these relations further. The Nasi’s mother issued a death threat, a proclamation of honour or religious murder, in order to ensure that the Nasi remained the boy she had foreseen achieving great deeds. One of the Nasi’s brothers had remarked the same, and abandoned the Nasi as well.

5 · The Walls Close In

With the walls closing in, the Dama sought escape before the inevitable. Many individuals sought the Dama’s demise, and some affiliated with the Democratic Labour Party openly declared that the Dama would be murdered.

The Dama staged an impromptu protest against the President of Barbados, Jeffrey Bostic, outside the St. Ann’s base. There the Dama was issued a death threat, should the Dama record the conduct of a particular officer on public property. Cadets afterwards addressed the Dama with a slur, which demoralised the Dama.

The Dama became outspoken against the Government, the political establishment and the Jewish community, issuing demands and petitioning the National Trust, and requesting governmental intervention against the possession of the Dama’s ancestors’ graves and the consequent deprivation of them from the Dama’s community.

The Dama became notorious, and was universally unwelcome across all political parties and platforms, each of which came into conflict with the Dama over secularism or queer rights.

Members of the New National Party called the Dama a devil for stating that Barbados “does not need more G-d” in governance, and declared the Dama afraid of G-d for censoring the name.

The Friends of Democracy silenced the Dama when the Dama openly stated that the Dama would not participate in Christian prayers, although Steve Blackett deeply desired the Dama’s presence and participation. The conduct of the President, the husband of Karina Greenidge, and of others within the party, marked by xenophobia, overzealousness and bigotry, threw the Dama into disrepute and then into social exile.

It seems that the Friends of Democracy wanted the Dama’s skills but not the mandate of protecting the vulnerable. Whereas other queer people had support networks, in EQUALS, in friends or in a community, the Dama stood alone in a political war that the Dama was badly losing.

6 · Advocacy for Refugees

The Nasi campaigned for the recognition of refugees and for their protection within Barbados. With funds secured from an old ally, Tyra Trotman, the Nasi attempted to feed a Russian refugee, a member of the Jewish Community whom the Nasi had met in person at Nidhe Israel, and petitioned Marsha Caddle on his behalf.

The Nasi also drew up a list of actionable steps to protect migrants and refugees and to advance progressive immigration policy, for recall among the Barbadian diaspora.

7 · Campaigns and Treatises

The Nasi published several media campaigns on the history of the Nasi’s community and on Spinozism. The Nasi threatened the Government with a self-destructive protest unless it ended the “cultural genocide of her people,” and openly wrote treatises against figures such as Ralph Thorne and institutions such as the Barbados Jewish Community.

8 · The Question of Israel

The Nasi was vocal in support of the Israeli people, openly breaking the norms of Barbadian culture and media, as a Jewish minority, to speak against the erosion of the rights of dual nationals and Israelis within or on the island.

Caribbean Against Apartheid in Palestine (CAAP) pursued political objectives that reached beyond the courtroom: that Barbados recognise the State of Palestine and cease to validate Israel diplomatically. Through a motion before the High Court, brought by the Caribbean Movement for Peace and Integration and led by CAAP’s secretary, it sought declarations that Israel is committing genocide, war crimes and crimes against humanity and operates an apartheid regime; a declaration that the Director of Public Prosecutions’ failure to act breached the Genocide Act and Article 11 of the Constitution; strict screening of Israeli passport holders at every port of entry; and the prosecution or deportation of persons involved, regardless of diplomatic status.

A note on the wording. In the Nasi’s analysis, the motion effectively criminalised Israeli presence in Barbados. Israelis are conscripted into military service, so a motion that sought the prosecution or deportation of anyone “involved,” in language left vague, could reach any Israeli who has served. That vagueness is why the wording of the motion was significant.

The motion also rested, in the Nasi’s assessment, on erroneous claims about Hamas and Israeli security policy. The Nasi spoke publicly to the media and was subsequently censored.

The Nasi appears holding a Palestinian flag in a photograph by the Starcom Network. The Nasi states that this was done under coercion.

The Nation later reported the motion dismissed. In the photograph the Nation published, the Dama appears smiling while others look solemn. The Dama was elated that the lawsuit had been thrown out, and could not contain it.

9 · L’Chaim in Exile

On the eve of the Nasi’s queer awakening, at the beginning of the Nasi’s Jewish awakening, the Nasi wrote L’Chaim in Exile.

10 · The Departure

During the Nasi’s escape from Barbados, immigration and customs officials ambushed the Nasi together with a Sky Cop from Interpol. The Nasi’s passport was seized, and the Nasi was interrogated and provoked as though the officers were attempting to catch the Nasi in a lie. In Barbados it is a crime to deceive immigration authorities, and a charge of that kind would have prevented the Nasi from reaching Amsterdam. The officials nonetheless had to release the Nasi, begrudgingly, for they had no legal justification to detain the Nasi, or none that they could prove.

The Nasi was investigated and interrogated in England, and cleared to continue the journey. The Nasi’s treatment and political enemies in Barbados became the focus of that questioning, and the Nasi notes that the British treated the Nasi with the respect and dignity that the Barbadians did not.

The Nasi was then imprisoned at the Schiphol detention centre, and won a legal victory against the Dutch state concerning the right to private life, after the Nasi’s hormonal regime was wilfully neglected.

11 · After the Escape

After the Nasi’s release, Barbados proclaimed new border-monitoring laws, aimed at detecting anyone planning to exit and re-enter the country. In the Nasi’s assessment, they closed the loophole the Nasi had used, and ensured that the authorities would be aware whenever the Nasi returned, should the Nasi ever do so.

For around a month the Nasi passed the days in fear, intimidated by all that had occurred before arriving in Amsterdam. The Nasi reasoned that Barbados was too concerned with its farcical diplomatic image to act at that juncture, and so continued the dissidence in exile, escalating in sophistication, to bring attention to the treatment of all the Nasi’s peoples.

The Nasi left the island before being able to ensure that the funds raised for the refugee reached him. Soon thereafter, a Russian diplomat shook hands with Santia Bradshaw.
